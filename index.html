Brand tracking & funnel analysis

Multi-market brand health & full-funnel conversion tracking

A multi-market tracking study measuring brand health, mental availability and movement through the purchase funnel for a global athletic footwear brand.

Scope
5 European markets
Category
Athletic footwear
Method
Quantitative tracking

Overview & objective

Evaluated digital brand health, mental availability and full-funnel conversion (awareness through consideration, preference and past-12-month purchase) across five key European markets, as part of a broader 12-country global tracker for a major athletic footwear and apparel brand. Performance was benchmarked against four primary global competitors among active sports consumers aged 18–65.

The challenge

Identify where conversion was breaking down across markets, and why regional consideration wasn't consistently translating into purchase relative to competitors.

My role

I co-led the quantitative analysis and was the primary author of the final report, analysing funnel performance, comparing markets, and translating the results into clear commercial recommendations for regional stakeholders.

Research approach

  • Sample & target: active athletic participants aged 18–65 across five European markets, part of a wider 12-country global tracker.
  • Framework: full-funnel measurement from awareness through past-12-month purchase, benchmarked against competitors.
  • Analysis: funnel diagnostics, competitor benchmarking, and double-indexed buying-occasion association mapping.

From data to insight

Funnel conversionAwareness alone didn't explain differences in market performance. The larger gaps opened further down the funnel.

1

2
Revitalise the brand narrative

Guide regional teams on revitalising core brand assets specifically in markets showing the sharpest consideration-to-purchase friction.

Digital UX & longitudinal research

Longitudinal product experience & digital ecosystem UX

A two-week study exploring how people actually used a health-tech device and its companion app in daily life, from the moment a need triggered use through to continued adoption.

Study
2-week home use test
Sample
N=101
Methods
Diary study + focus groups

Overview & objective

Evaluated real-world use of a personal recovery device and its digital ecosystem for a global health-technology brand. The longitudinal study mapped the full user journey over two weeks to inform interface requirements and companion-app strategy.

The challenge

Understand how people actually used the device and companion software over time, what drove adherence versus drop-off across different user segments, and translate those behavioural patterns into concrete digital UI and onboarding requirements.

My role

I was a co-researcher on a multi-disciplinary team: contributing to questionnaire and task design for the tracking platform, running quantitative data-quality checks, carrying out descriptive behavioural analysis, and co-authoring the product requirements report. I also built the user journey bringing together the different stages of the experience.

User journey: from trigger to continued use

The experience was mapped around the moments that mattered most to users: what triggered use, how they learned the product, what happened during repeated use, how it fitted into their routine, and what ultimately shaped continued use.

Study design

Phase 1 was a two-week home-use test with 53 participants, using a mobile diary app to capture repeated use, session behaviour, mode selection and satisfaction. Phase 2 was eight focus groups with 48 participants, used to explore experiences in more depth and understand the reasons behind behaviours observed during the home-use period.

From data to insight

  • The learning curve: satisfaction rose over successive uses once people found their own comfort threshold, pointing to a need for guided digital onboarding.
  • Terminology vs. mental models: technical program names caused hesitation; people overwhelmingly preferred outcome-based language matched to their goal.
  • A nuanced app opportunity: interest in a companion app was strong for remote control and saving presets, but making it mandatory created an adoption barrier for less tech-oriented users.

Impact & recommendations

  • UI specification: move to outcome-based mode labelling, and add a clear lock mechanism to prevent accidental mode changes mid-session.
  • Digital ecosystem strategy: build an optional companion app focused on guided setup and remote adjustment, while keeping the core product fully usable standalone.

Concept & price testing

Monadic concept testing & price optimization

A quantitative concept test evaluating a new product against established competitors, paired with price-sensitivity modelling to find the revenue-maximising launch price.

Sample
N=1,200 (2 markets)
Method
Monadic test + Gabor-Granger
Standard
ISO 20252

Overview & objective

Evaluated a new product concept combining an emerging category format with premium digital features against two established market competitors, across two European markets. The study validated commercial viability, purchase intent and revenue-maximising price points ahead of a full rollout.

The challenge

Determine whether the concept met pre-registered commercial performance benchmarks against top competitors, and identify the price-elasticity point that maximised total modelled revenue in each market.

My role

I built custom survey modules within the standardised template, ran statistical significance testing at 90% and 95% confidence, interpreted the Gabor-Granger pricing outputs, led data quality checks, and co-authored the final insight report.

Research approach

  • Sample & target: monadic quantitative concept test (n=200 per concept per market; N=1,200 total) across two European markets, run to ISO 20252 standards.
  • Monadic benchmarking: three concepts tested independently (one client prototype vs. two established competitor products) against pre-registered win/parity/lose standards on core attributes, purchase intent and value perception.
  • Price sensitivity: a Gabor-Granger pricing ladder per concept measured purchase likelihood across incremental price tiers.

From data to insight

  • Significance testing: ran significance testing at 90% and 95% confidence across concepts and sub-segments to ensure reported gaps reflected real market differences rather than sampling noise.
  • Revenue curve modelling: converted Gabor-Granger purchase-likelihood scores into modelled demand curves to pinpoint the price tier that maximised revenue.
  • Divergent elasticity: in Market A, a lower price point drove higher total modelled revenue. In Market B, price was already close to optimal, so underperformance came from feature positioning, not price.

Impact & recommendations

  • Market A: lower the target launch price to capture higher volume and maximise total modelled revenue.
  • Market B: hold the tested price point and refocus messaging on the concept's core functional advantage.

Usability & concept evaluation

Product usability & interface concept evaluation

Evaluating early-stage design concepts for a new consumer device and its digital interface, benchmarked against established market competitors.

Sample
2 groups, n=10
Scope
4 concepts vs 3 competitors
Method
Quant scorecard + qual moderation

Overview & objective

Evaluated early-stage design concepts for a new consumer device and digital interface, benchmarking four prototype variations against three established market competitors to guide engineering and design investment.

The challenge

Determine which design best balanced ergonomics, control usability and consumer appeal across current category users and prospective non-users, while identifying trade-offs against established market alternatives.

My role

I owned the study end-to-end: wrote the screener and recruitment logic, developed the moderation guide, designed the in-session quantitative scorecard, tabulated the dataset, and authored the final executive report.

Research approach

  • Sample & target: in-person focus group sessions (n=10), stratified by category experience: current product users vs. non-users.
  • Mixed-methods design: hands-on testing of four concepts and three competitor benchmarks, paired with a custom six-attribute scorecard (1–7 rating scales, ranking tasks, purchase intent, willingness-to-pay).
  • Analysis: descriptive statistics (attribute means, ranking distributions, purchase-intent proportions) used to structure and ground the qualitative feedback.

From data to insight

Metrics meet mechanicsPaired scorecard scores with qualitative feedback, tracing why one prototype scored highest on perceived intuitiveness (a raised interface layout) while another led on comfort.
Segment-driven prioritiesExisting users favoured precise control logic, while non-users prioritised comfort and ease of initial handling.
Methodological rigourTreated scorecard metrics as directional signal appropriate for small-sample qualitative validation, rather than over-claiming statistical precision.

Impact & recommendations

1
Hybrid product direction

Advised against selecting a single prototype as tested; recommended combining the highest-scoring control interface, body silhouette and comfort cues from three separate concepts into a unified design.

2
Segment-specific strategy

Formulated targeted value propositions: feature-clarity messaging for non-users, performance-upgrade cues for existing category users.

Skills demonstrated

End-to-end research ownership · Custom quantitative scorecard design · Usability evaluation · Data tabulation & descriptive analysis · Mixed-methods synthesis

Claim validation & packaging

Quantitative packaging design & on-pack claim validation

Measuring how quickly consumers understood and recalled packaging claims for a functional dairy innovation.

Sample
N=255
Category
FMCG innovation
Method
Micro-timed tasks

Overview & objective

Evaluated new packaging designs and value-proposition messaging for a brand innovation ahead of launch, measuring visibility, visual hierarchy, claim comprehension and purchase intent against key market competitors (n=255).

The challenge

Determine whether the front-of-pack claim clearly communicated the functional product benefit, without causing confusion with adjacent brand lines or creating cognitive friction through technical terminology.

My role

I worked on custom questionnaire modules, quantitative QA, analysis and insight development. I also contributed to the insights deck and independently presented the findings to brand leadership.

Research approach

  • Sample & target: quantitative evaluation (n=255) of category buyers and non-buyers.
  • Testing methodology: sequential monadic testing with timed exposure tasks, diagnostic visual mapping, and unprompted message recall.
  • Benchmarking: performance compared directly against two established category competitors.

From data to insight

  • Micro-timed recall: complex, technical wording created cognitive friction and depressed message comprehension relative to plain language.
  • Diagnostic mapping: analysis of spatial feedback flagged visual overlap and brand-cannibalisation risk with existing adjacent product lines.
  • Translating data to design: converted eye-flow and diagnostic metrics into explicit design rules: plain-language claims combined with clear visual anchors improved readability.

Impact & recommendations

  • Plain-language messaging achieved substantially higher unprompted recall (74%) and trust than technical phrasing (38%), providing a clear direction for the on-pack proposition.
  • Delivered concrete pre-launch guidelines (typography adjustments, colour-coding rules and simplified copy) enabling the team to finalise artwork.
  • Independently presented the insights to brand management and executive leadership.
